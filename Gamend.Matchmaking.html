    Gamend.Matchmaking (gamend_core v1.1.1392)

    Copy Markdown View Source

    Public API for the built-in matchmaking system.

    Matchmaking is ticket-based. Each call to join/4 creates a ticket in the database. The periodic Gamend.Matchmaking.Worker groups queued tickets that share the same match_params and creates a hidden lobby for each match.

    Tickets are the source of truth; there is no cached queue. The sweep reads them directly through the (status, queued_at) index, and the worker serializes the sweep cluster-wide so only one instance forms matches at a time.

    Liveness

    A queued ticket only means something while its owner can still be pulled into a lobby. Two things keep the queue honest:

    • UserChannel.terminate/2 cancels the user's tickets when their socket closes, and
    • prune_offline/0 sweeps tickets whose owner the database no longer considers online — the safety net for tickets created over HTTP by a client that never opened a socket.

    Backfill

    A lobby's host who queues fills that lobby: the ticket carries its lobby_id, and a match seats the other players there rather than in a new lobby. A match holds at most one lobby with other players in it, and fits in its seats; a player queued from a lobby of their own alone leaves it for the match's. With no lobby among them, a match gets a new hidden lobby, as before.

    Accept

    accept_timeout_ms (a join/5 option, or the before_matchmaking_join hook's attrs) makes a formed match ask first: a ready check of kind accept over its players (Gamend.ReadyChecks, subject :matchmaking). Nobody is seated until everyone accepts. A decline or the deadline fails it: whoever accepted, or had not answered when another declined, goes back in the queue, keeping their place, and the others' tickets are cancelled. Cancelling a ticket (cancel/1, a socket closing) declines the accept its holder was asked for. A player who declined may queue again at once (a game's Skip that keeps looking): their declined ticket no longer counts as queued, though it is cancelled only once the check settles.

    @spec cancel(Ecto.UUID.t()) :: non_neg_integer()

    Cancels the user's queued tickets. Returns how many were cancelled.

    A party queues as a unit, so it leaves as one: cancelling any member's ticket cancels the whole party's. Any member may do this — only the leader can queue, but nobody should be stuck in a queue they cannot leave.

    A match waiting for its accept is declined for the user (see Accept), so the others go back in the queue at once rather than at its deadline.

    claim(tickets)

    @spec claim([Gamend.Matchmaking.Ticket.t()]) ::
  {:ok, [Gamend.Matchmaking.Ticket.t()]} | :conflict

    Atomically claims a formed match: flips its tickets from queued to matched.

    Runs inside the sweep's cluster lock, before any lobby exists (the lobby is created outside the lock because Lobbies fires hooks and broadcasts, which must not run inside a transaction). The status == queued guard makes the claim atomic: if any ticket was cancelled or claimed since it was read, nothing is claimed and :conflict is returned.

    Answers the tickets as claimed. Their matched_at is this claim's mark: requeue/1, discard/1 and assign_lobby/2 write only tickets that still carry it, so a claim only ever undoes or names what it holds itself.

    join(user, match_params, min_players \\ nil, max_players \\ nil, opts \\ [])

    @spec join(
  Gamend.Accounts.User.t(),
  map(),
  pos_integer() | nil,
  pos_integer() | nil,
  keyword()
) ::
  {:ok, Gamend.Matchmaking.Ticket.t()} | {:error, Ecto.Changeset.t() | atom()}

    Adds a user — or their whole party — to the matchmaking queue.

    match_params is a map of arbitrary string keys and values, for example %{"mode" => "deathmatch", "map" => "dust2"}. Only tickets with exactly the same parameters are matched together.

    min_players and max_players can be passed to override the defaults. opts: accept_timeout_ms, the time a formed match gives its players to accept (see Accept); none seats them at once.

    A lobby's host queues to fill that lobby (see Backfill).

    Parties

    A user in a party cannot queue alone: only the leader may queue, and doing so creates one ticket per member sharing the party's id. Those tickets are matched as an indivisible unit. Because only the leader queues, every member's ticket carries the same limits by construction — there is no way for members to disagree about min_players/max_players.

    Returns the caller's own ticket. Fails with:

    • {:error, :not_party_leader} — a member tried to queue
    • {:error, :party_too_large} — the party cannot fit in max_players
    • {:error, :party_has_blocked_pair} — two members have blocked each other
    • {:error, :already_queued} — the caller or a party member is queued

    prune_offline()

    @spec prune_offline() :: non_neg_integer()

    Cancels queued tickets whose owner has been offline past the grace period.

    Reads users.is_online — the flag the user channel maintains — instead of taking a caller-supplied id list, so it stays correct on any node. Going offline is not enough on its own: the player must have been gone longer than matchmaking_offline_grace_ms, so a brief disconnect does not cost a queue position. A player who queued over HTTP and never opened a socket has no last_seen_at, so their queued_at starts the same grace period.

    A party is matched as a unit, so it is pruned as one: if any member times out, the whole party leaves the queue. Returns how many tickets were cancelled.
